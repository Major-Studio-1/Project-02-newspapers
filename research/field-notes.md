## Scope and disposition

Two targeted searches examined both OCR fields across all six local Parquet shards at the pinned dataset revision. These were date-bounded searches, not searches for every historical falsehood. The second pass broadened spelling and neighboring phrase patterns.

## What the evidence supports

1. **Boyd message:** corresponding core passages appear in the Patowmac Guardian (March 16), New Hampshire Gazette (March 25), and Kentucky Gazette (April 12), 1797. One has an appended CHRONICLE challenge. The 27-day span is an observed publication interval, not a measured journey time. A Kentucky account on April 19 describes the fleet returning to Brest, but does not explicitly retract the earlier message. The phrase “must inevitably be captured” is a prediction, not a statement that capture had already occurred.
2. **Peace / Cayenne:** a May 18 Gazette of the United States report and a May 30 New Hampshire Gazette version share a seizure account attributed to Captain Whitney. The latter includes extra commentary attributed to Mercury. A June 27 translated decree and commentary cites American newspapers and alleges that seven of eight cited examples are false. The originals and a complete itemized check remain missing. The seizure reports and decree are related context, not a verified causal chain.
3. **1794 resolution:** the Mirrour (May 19) and New Hampshire Gazette (May 20) preserve the main clearance provisions. The approval date, eligibility terms, and security/no-unloading condition are supported by [Statutes at Large, vol. 1, p. 401, Resolution V](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/llsl/llsl-c3/llsl-c3.pdf#page=61). This comparison verifies document content, not implementation.

## Why a fake-news rate is not reported

The 15-claim ledger distinguishes factual reports, predictions, challenges, and accusations. The sample is purposive; independent adjudication and a representative denominator are lacking. “Unknown” is neither true nor false. A hostile editor's allegation does not establish deliberate deception. Multiple printed copies do not count as independent corroborating witnesses.

Four pairs are used to compare neighboring observed versions within three message groups. None establishes a direct parent–child copying route. Therefore an omitted annotation cannot yet be counted as lost uncertainty during copying. Correction delay and coverage also remain unestimated, because explicit corrections and complete observation windows have not been established.

## OCR finding

The RevX text for the March 16 Patowmac passage says “distressed”; the scan reads “dismasted,” matching the other reviewed versions. This apparent change is an OCR/transcription artifact. Excerpts retain their source transcription, with scan corrections recorded separately. Several older transcriptions also render long-s “sailed” as “failed.”

## Limits and next step

All review was Codex-assisted; there is no independent second-reader adjudication. Ship names, speakers and boundaries require human review. The next bounded task is one message family with an independent second annotation and a search for explicit named-source copies and relevant original archives. Stop expanding a truth metric if source documents remain unavailable.

The current relation prototype is a local, filtered circular view linked to dates and evidence. A graph can organize observations without being a trained neural network. Predicting future coverage would require a target, temporal and reprint-family holdouts, and baseline comparison. Recomputing a graph after deleting an edge is sensitivity analysis, not a historical causal intervention. Artistic sound or weaving can be a separate declared mapping.
